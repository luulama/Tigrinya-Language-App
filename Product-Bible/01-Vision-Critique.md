# Section 1 — Critical Review

**Reviewed:** First version of `01-Vision.md`, 20 November 2026.  
**Perspectives:** CEO, investor, learning scientist, product strategist, and child education specialist. These are analytical perspectives, not a claim that external experts reviewed the document.  
**Verdict:** Strong ethical intentions; insufficient strategic commitment. The document explains how to behave responsibly more clearly than it explains which problem the company will solve first, why families will choose it, or how it will survive delivering that promise.

The original does several things well: it respects Tigrinya, rejects invented content and manipulative engagement, distinguishes assisted performance from learning, and recognises human review as essential. Those protections should survive. The rewrite must make them operational without turning the vision into a detailed roadmap or pretending demand and effectiveness have been validated.

## 1. CEO critique — The mission does not force enough choices

The mission spans literacy, communication, children, families, adults, educators, cultural participation, and eventual multilingual expansion. Saying “children first” does not resolve which child, with which prior language knowledge, receives the first complete experience.

“Earn trust through a small, coherent learning experience” is sensible but incomplete. Small in what dimension? What observable result earns trust? What would make the team stop building more screens and fix the teaching?

**Risk:** Every attractive feature can claim alignment. An infrastructure-heavy team can keep building while the minimum teachable course remains unfinished.

**Required correction:** Select an initial need, distinguish the child user from the adult decision-maker, name a bounded learning promise, and make expansion conditional on evidence and operating capacity. Assign decision responsibilities and escalation rules without inventing staffed roles.

## 2. Investor critique — There is no investable or fundable operating thesis yet

“Sustainable care” is a value, not an explanation of sustainability. The document leaves acquisition, willingness to pay, funding, reviewer costs, media rights, support burden, and maintenance almost entirely unspecified.

There is no evidence here establishing market size, willingness to pay, acquisition cost, or suitability for venture funding. A worthy mission does not establish any of those. Broad educational ambition can hide a business whose content costs rise as quickly as revenue.

**Risk:** A beautiful subsidised prototype is mistaken for a repeatable service. Funding runs out before the reviewed course and support obligations become sustainable.

**Required correction:** State an initial adult value proposition and a testable distribution hypothesis. Measure cost and time per complete released objective, correction burden, and acquisition/support cost. Keep commercial viability, social value, and venture-scale potential as separate questions. Do not invent prices, market figures, or a revenue forecast.

## 3. Learning scientist critique — The outcomes are sound in principle but under-specified

“Growth in independently demonstrated, curriculum-defined Tigrinya capability” is too elastic to discipline a product. The same phrase could describe selecting a familiar symbol or reading unfamiliar combinations. The original correctly rejects misleading mastery scores but does not replace them with a sufficiently clear evidence hierarchy.

“Teach before testing” risks becoming an inflexible ritual if it prevents a learner from showing prior knowledge or practising retrieval. “One goal” risks becoming a sequence of isolated fragments if connected meaning is deferred indefinitely. More scaffolding can make the screen easier while leaving the learner dependent on the scaffold.

General evidence supports spacing, worked examples, and retrieval in particular contexts. It does not establish this app's ideal intervals, lesson duration, or Tigrinya sequence. [IES instruction and study guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1)

**Risk:** Improvement reflects repeated items, pictures, answer positions, or immediate imitation. Before-and-after gains are attributed to the app without accounting for maturation, outside teaching, or missing follow-up.

**Required correction:** Specify immediate, delayed, and parallel-task evidence; document support; remove irrelevant cues; distinguish usability evaluation from learning evaluation and causal effectiveness research. Set advancement thresholds before examining results, with appropriate expertise, rather than manufacturing precision in a vision document.

## 4. Product strategist critique — Differentiation is mostly a collection of virtues

Accuracy, clarity, accessibility, and kind feedback are valuable requirements. They are also easy claims for another product to make. The original does not show which capabilities must work together to provide distinctive value.

A plausible strategic position is a reviewed bridge from familiar spoken Tigrinya to early Fidäl literacy, with low adult orchestration. That is a hypothesis to test. It is more specific than building a better version of every feature in a large language app.

The original also leaves a contradiction unresolved: the adult beginner with little Tigrinya and the heritage child with substantial spoken understanding may both see the same beginner path. Simplifying appearance does not resolve differences in language knowledge.

**Risk:** One generic course underserves several audiences. The multilingual architecture becomes the strategy, while language-specific pedagogy becomes an exception.

**Required correction:** Choose a starting learner situation, preserve a route for other learners without promising equivalent readiness, and make reviewed content plus teaching quality plus correction operations the distinctive system. Validate a small audience before adding language packs or parallel curricula.

## 5. Child education critique — Ages 4–8 is not one readiness level

The document acknowledges differences but does not adequately address oral vocabulary, interface comprehension, motor control, shared-device use, or the gap between supported use and independence. A young child who misunderstands the prompt may receive a false language failure. A child who recognises a picture may receive a false reading success.

Independent app use must not become the definition of educational success. Adult–child interaction is itself valuable in early language learning; EEF's early-years guidance emphasises its quality. That does not prove a specific app design, but it challenges treating adult involvement purely as friction. [EEF Preparing for Literacy](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/literacy-early-years)

**Risk:** The product mistakes developmentally inappropriate tasks for low ability, encourages excessive screen practice, or excludes families who cannot provide language coaching.

**Required correction:** Route by relevant abilities and support needs, make the child able to pause or decline, distinguish technical supervision from teaching assistance, and offer meaningful off-screen participation. Do not score motor precision or speech as language competence without a validated basis.

## 6. Inconsistencies and missing elements

| Weakness or tension | Consequence | Stronger decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Literacy first, but mission promises broad communication. | Implied scope exceeds the first teachable product. | Foundation literacy is the first promise; communication is its purpose and longer-term direction. |
| Independent use, but reviewed spoken guidance is missing. | The product can overclaim readiness for non-readers. | Independent use is conditional on required assets and observed use. |
| “All two-character words” alongside small-step teaching. | Catalogue completeness can displace suitability. | Retain the full sourced bank for review; teach a selected approved sequence. |
| No pressure, but little account of motivation. | A calm product may still be repetitive or dull. | Build enjoyment through agency, meaningful challenge, stories, and visible capability. |
| Universal accessibility aspiration without tested boundaries. | Excluded learners become invisible. | Name supported paths, test alternatives, and report unresolved barriers. |
| Review required, but no bottleneck or dispute strategy. | One expert can block publication or become sole authority. | Budget review capacity, independent approval, second opinions, and correction operations. |
| Privacy versus longitudinal evaluation. | Useful evidence may become excessive tracking. | Separate optional evaluation from ordinary learning and collect only justified evidence. |
| Ten-year ambition without disconfirming conditions. | The vision is impossible to falsify. | Define when to narrow, pause, or redesign. |

## 7. Scalability critique — The constraint is not mainly server capacity

The difficult scaling unit is a complete, accurate, usable learning objective with licensed media, review, accessibility, and correction support. Counting words or generated exercises hides those costs.

Human review introduces capacity and consistency risks. Regional variation introduces governance decisions. Offline distribution introduces correction and versioning obligations. Each new language introduces new expertise requirements. None is solved merely by adding content types to a database.

**Required correction:** Scale reusable approved content and dependable authoring operations before learner-facing breadth. Preserve exact-version approvals, auditability, and the ability to withdraw material. Fund operational capacity before expanding the promise.

## 8. Changes made in the rewrite

The replacement keeps the eight requested vision areas and the original ethical commitments. It adds a proposed starting audience, a bounded learner promise, an adult value proposition, a theory of change, an evidence hierarchy, ownership rules, sustainability tests, risk controls, and conditions for expansion or redesign.

The choices remain proposed strategy for owner review. They do not create subscriptions, collect child data, approve Tigrinya content, change the live app, or supersede existing repository instructions automatically. Detailed curricula, activity systems, business strategy, and roadmap remain unwritten here.
