**Boundary:** This Bible specifies what to author, teach, review, and evaluate. It does not invent Tigrinya language, approve assets, certify proficiency, or authorise implementation.

## 1. Educational mandate and audience

The first course connects understood spoken meaning with early Tigrinya reading and writing for the primary child situation ages 4–8. The intended learner is not defined by age alone: establish target meaning, oral knowledge, script experience, interaction understanding, and access separately. A child needing oral-language foundations receives meaning support and an honest boundary, not a false literacy failure.

Broader beginner, heritage, advanced, and professional learning is fully represented in the programme architecture but is not simultaneously promised at launch. Parent, educator, and institutional needs do not override learner dignity, stopping, access, or truthful evidence. See PB01, PB03, and PB18.

## 2. Canonical programme structure

Use Course → Stage → Unit → Module → Lesson, with reusable objectives, language content, stories/dialogues, activities, reviews, projects, and checkpoints referenced rather than copied. PB05's 32 named planning units cover the eight stages. The A module establishes meaning/models and focused relationships; B supports purposeful practice, correction, and later/parallel use. Module/lesson counts vary with educational needs.

| Stage | Principal curriculum responsibility | Sample unit destination from PB05 | Required evidence focus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PL | Meaning, participation, relevant sound/form and print exploration. | Understand a familiar situation; explore print and marks. | Interpretable response, with support explicit. |
| ER | Selected Fidäl relationships, suitable words, beginning writing. | Fidäl Foundations; First Words. | Distinguish visual recognition, sound mapping, reading, and writing. |
| BG | Purposeful familiar exchanges and short messages. | A short story or meaningful message. | Appropriate changed detail after teaching. |
| EL | Connected everyday sequence, clarification, and revision. | Personal account or connected message. | Comprehension and production criteria separately. |
| IN | Explanation, information exchange, and problem-solving. | A comparison or retelling project. | Organisation, meaning, relevant accuracy, and repair. |
| UI | Sources, justification, register, and responsive discussion. | Source-aware choice/explanation. | Evidence interpretation and audience adaptation. |
| AD | Nuance, extended discourse, independent source use, revision. | Extended portfolio across tasks. | Qualified rubrics and appropriately varied evidence. |
| PR | Bounded domain communication and clarification. | Briefing, professional message, presentation/questions, interview. | Domain-appropriate task evidence, no licence/certification claim. |

Stages are not age ranks or automatic CEFR equivalences. Workload envelopes in PB04 are planning assumptions, not Tigrinya acquisition rates or child screen-time instructions. Placement and advancement remain per skill.

## 3. Objective authoring contract

Every concrete objective must contain: stable ID and language; capability and skill node from PB06; intended purpose; target meaning/sense; understood language/readiness; hard prerequisites and acceptable reviewed alternatives; source content/model revisions; teachable contrast or pattern; activity and response condition; feedback; access requirements; criterion/rubric; later and parallel opportunity where claimed; required review scopes; owner; and release dependencies.

Write the capability as something observable in a task. “Learn this word” is too broad. “Interpret the approved written sense under a specified cue condition” is narrower and permits fair assessment. Do not label a multiple-choice response as spontaneous speech or handwriting.

Critical criteria cannot be compensated by an average. Supporting criteria can guide feedback, but the checkpoint must preserve an unassessed critical skill. A learner may explore suitable material with support while assessment readiness remains incomplete.

## 4. Source and language production

For each candidate, retain original form, distinct sense, source entry/page, attribution, rights, context, variety, date where relevant, and uncertainty. Use qualified Tigrinya contributions for all learner-facing words, translations, transliterations, grammar examples, prompts, models, and cultural associations.

The existing dictionary bank remains 142 sourced draft candidates. A subset of 32 picture candidates and a full 293-form preview are not automatically approved course content. Unit 2 retains exactly two Ethiopic letter graphemes per candidate; do not truncate or invent examples. The writer records why a candidate is unsuitable or blocked, rather than deleting its source history.

Pronunciation requires clear licensed native recordings with exact-language/context review. A transliteration is an optional explicitly reviewed support policy, not the source of pronunciation truth. Writing sequence claims require reliable teaching sources and qualified verification; a font outline cannot supply them.

## 5. The complete authoring pack

| Pack component | Required material | Release consequence if absent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objective sheet | Capability, purpose, critical/supporting criteria, prerequisites and alternatives. | Cannot claim curriculum coverage. |
| Language sheet | Exact reviewed forms, senses, patterns, acceptable variants and sources. | Dependent language tasks held. |
| Native model sheet | Recording/transcript/segments, permissions, context and quality. | Sound-dependent/independent-nonreader claims held. |
| Visual sheet | Relevant meaning association, rights, alt description, image/context review. | Do not substitute a convenient ambiguous image. |
| Writing sheet | Correct form and, where taught, verified stroke model/static alternative. | Shape-only alternative must be labelled honestly. |
| Activity sheet | Mechanism, conditions, response, support, feedback, safe exit, recovery. | A content item alone is not a complete lesson. |
| Assessment sheet | Key/rubric, criterion coverage, evidence spans, support, parallel set, rater guidance. | No mastery or checkpoint inference. |
| Review sheet | Scope/qualification, author independence, exact revision/hash, decision and date. | No learner publication. |
| Release sheet | Approved version dependencies, language/variety, correction and withdrawal owner. | No uncontrolled bundle distribution. |

Approvals bind the complete association, not just isolated words. Replacing an image, recording, answer, or model can alter the teaching. PB15's change-impact class decides which scopes and dependent tasks must be re-reviewed.

## 6. Foundation lesson pack: a worked specification

**Planning objective:** Connect an already understood spoken word with its approved two-grapheme written form and investigate later reading under controlled cues. This is an assignment template, not an authored Tigrinya lesson.

**Prerequisites:** Target sense understood; task action demonstrated; approved word/form mapping, native recording, visual association, response key and accessible controls present. If meaning is unknown, establish it first and label that evidence separately.

**First encounter:** Present a reviewed meaningful situation, show the written form with native model, focus one useful relation, demonstrate the response, and offer guided practice with replay/help. Correct specifically and allow another try. Keep the approximate 3–5 minute design target flexible; no countdown.

**Follow-up:** Offer an appropriate independent attempt before answer help and a later suitable opportunity. Use a reviewed parallel presentation/context with controlled new demands. A picture-based success remains supported association if the picture supplies the reading answer.

**Writing extension:** Offer suitable copy/shape work only if accurate models exist. A memory-writing or stroke-order claim requires its own valid task and review. Do not inherit a reading score.

**Conclusion:** Say what was practised and provide one optional next step or stop. Save only eligible evidence within explicit storage scope. Missing media, refusal, interruption, and a failed save must not become language errors or fictitious progress.

## 7. Modality and connected-use responsibilities

Stories need target objectives, language demands, reference/sequence, sources, segments, rights, mode-specific supports, questions, and all authored branches reviewed. Read-along is supported reading; audio-only interpretation remains listening. See PB09.

Speaking needs meaning, appropriate native models, prepared and later contingent opportunities, specific feedback, and qualified criteria. Rehearsal does not prove conversation, and speech-recognition confidence is not pronunciation truth. See PB10.

Listening needs approved audio and an objective-specific replay/transcript policy. Test gist, detail, reference, inference, or repair as appropriate, without letting written cues answer a listening question. See PB11.

Writing distinguishes shape exploration, tracing, copying, memory writing, composition, and revision, with input mode explicit. Open text may have several valid responses and needs qualified/descriptive feedback. See PB12.

Culture contextualises meaning and perspectives, not identity conformity. Historical claims, humour, variants, and media rights may require additional expertise. No learner must disclose private family beliefs/history. Cultural context's retention value is a hypothesis to evaluate. See PB13.

## 8. Review and criterion evidence

Use PB14's EVIDENCE_V1 and M0–M3 as proposed policy, not a scientific norm. Preserve support categories: access accommodation, interaction guidance, meaning/model help, answer-revealing help, feedback retry, and unknown support. Observed independence cannot prove absence of all outside help.

The proposed M3 rule requires three eligible successes on separate opportunities at least 24 hours apart, including one after a 7-day exposure-free gap and a reviewer-approved parallel success. Qualify its suitability and criterion coverage before consequential use. A baseline first success is not retention of app teaching. A corrected retry is practice, not another independent event.

For open outputs, define criterion anchors, rater qualification, agreement checks, reasons, and reconsideration. Do not impose meaningless precision. A progress view can truthfully show dated capability with context while leaving other skills unassessed.

## 9. Pilot and evaluation protocol responsibilities

Separate formative usability, learning change, attribution, and operating readiness. Predefine objectives, sample/context, tasks, support coding, follow-up, missingness, and analysis before collecting results. Include primary audience variation, non-fluent supporters, access alternatives, interruptions, and people who struggle or stop.

The internal 8-of-10 usability trigger is not an efficacy estimate. Candidate 7/30-day study intervals are not universal memory laws. A causal claim needs an appropriate comparator/design; do not advertise a supervised small pilot as proof of independent use across all ages.

Collect only justified information under a suitable permission/safeguarding process. Ordinary learning is not automatic research participation. No new study or recording is activated by this Bible.

## 10. Production capacity and release

The founder, developer, writer, and designer have different responsibilities. Obtain actual qualified independent review, native media contributors, and relevant access/evaluation expertise. Missing expertise blocks the affected claim; neither AI nor a job title substitutes for it.

Track source discovery, draft readiness, media completeness, review lead time, correction, dependencies, and maintenance cost per complete objective. Reuse approved assets where their intended association remains valid. Do not scale by duplicating exercises or shortening every word.

A unit is release-ready when its stated objectives have full required model/assessment/access coverage, exact-version approval and rights, tested delivery/recovery, and a suitable evidence/claims plan. A stage remains a planning framework until actual material exists.
