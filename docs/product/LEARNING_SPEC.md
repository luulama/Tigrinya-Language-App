# Learning and curriculum specification

## Stage contracts

Stage labels organise curriculum, not learner identity. Oral comprehension and production can advance while literacy remains at an earlier stage. Pre-literacy is a pathway, not an age-based ceiling. The course uses CEFR as a descriptor reference; its thresholds below are internal pilot criteria and do not confer CEFR certification. [CEFR Companion Volume](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-companion-volume-and-its-language-versions)

Vocabulary budgets count cumulative reviewed sense entries, with inflected surface forms linked to a lexeme rather than counted repeatedly. Targets are initial authoring budgets, not evidence-based Tigrinya proficiency cutoffs. Productive knowledge is assessed through relevant use; no fixed receptive/productive ratio is assumed.

| Stage | Learning and vocabulary target | Listening | Speaking | Reading | Writing | Grammar and culture objectives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P Pre-literacy and foundations | Understand familiar meanings; 50 sense entries | Identify named familiar objects/actions in supported contexts | Name familiar objects and respond with a learned expression or accessible alternative | Connect selected reviewed sounds, forms and word meanings | Make intentional marks; copy selected forms; optional verified tracing | Notice meaningful recurring expressions without rule terminology; recognise variation in everyday family practices |
| B Beginner | Complete simple personal exchanges; 700 sense entries | Understand short familiar instructions and exchanges | Ask/answer personal questions and request help | Decode introduced Fidäl and read short supported messages | Write familiar words and short useful messages | Reviewed basic statement, question and negative constructions; introductions and respectful interaction |
| E Elementary | Manage routine situations; 1,500 sense entries | Follow clear short narratives and transactions | Complete routine exchanges and short descriptions | Read short independent stories and practical information | Link simple sentences into descriptions and messages | Reviewed reference, agreement, location, quantity and time expressions; food, family and invitation contexts |
| I Intermediate | Narrate and explain; 3,000 sense entries | Identify main points and important details in connected speech | Recount events, explain choices and repair misunderstandings | Read graded extended narratives and accessible articles | Write connected accounts with an understandable sequence | Reviewed narrative sequencing, clause linking and reported information; compare community perspectives |
| U Upper intermediate | Discuss and defend positions; 5,000 sense entries | Follow extended multi-speaker discussion on familiar subjects | Sustain discussion, justify a view and negotiate a plan | Read varied articles, narrative and viewpoint texts | Write structured arguments, summaries and reports | Reviewed stance, qualification and complex discourse patterns; media and social-context interpretation |
| A Advanced | Communicate flexibly across registers; 8,000 sense entries | Interpret implication, speaker stance and complex organisation | Present complex material and adapt register to an audience | Critically read demanding literary and specialist texts | Produce coherent, precise extended work and revision | Reviewed register choices, cohesion and rhetorical variation; contextualised literary and historical perspectives |
| R Professional and advanced competency | Execute demanding domain tasks; 12,000+ sense entries as scope requires | Synthesise long, varied-source input including unfamiliar speakers | Facilitate meetings, debate nuance and answer challenging questions | Compare specialist sources and evaluate implicit positions | Produce polished professional/academic documents for an intended audience | Domain discourse and pragmatic precision reviewed by specialists; regional, institutional and ethical context |

Grammar rows commission a Tigrinya teaching inventory. They are not invented Tigrinya rules. A qualified specialist supplies the actual constructions, progression, examples, pronunciation and acceptable variants before any grammar activity becomes eligible for release.

### Exact internal graduation rules

An objective has four evidence dimensions where relevant: comprehension, production, delayed retrieval and transfer. A five-point rubric uses 0 for no relevant performance, 1 for extensive modelling, 2 for partially effective performance with significant support, 3 for independently effective performance with minor limitations, and 4 for flexible, clear performance. Accuracy is scored only against reviewed answers or a qualified assessor's rubric. Unavailable or inaccessible modalities are “not assessed” and have an accommodation path; they do not receive a false zero.

| Stage | Required evidence for each assessed skill |
| --- | --- |
| P | Identify 8 of 10 unfamiliar picture arrangements using taught meanings on each of two visits at least 7 days apart. Match 8 of 10 introduced sound/form targets where verified audio exists. Produce 4 of 5 relevant responses through speech or an agreed accessible response. Copy 4 of 5 taught forms recognisably against a reviewed model; this is a literacy sub-gate, not an oral-language gate. |
| B | On two task sets at least 7 days apart, score at least 8/10 comprehension questions, correctly decode 8/10 taught-pattern words in unseen arrangements and complete three changed-detail everyday exchanges at rubric 3 or above. Produce three short messages at rubric 3 or above for meaning and comprehensibility. |
| E | Meet 8/10 reading and 8/10 listening comprehension on two parallel sets; complete three routine exchanges with at least one unexpected clarification each; write two connected descriptions/messages. Each productive rubric dimension must be at least 3 on both visits. |
| I | Meet 8/10 comprehension on a narrative and a practical text in each modality; narrate one event, explain one choice and repair a misunderstanding; write one account and one explanation. Each required productive dimension is at least 3, with two sets at least 7 days apart. |
| U | Meet 8/10 comprehension on two different text genres per modality; participate in a discussion and negotiate a plan; write an argument and a report. Qualified human scores of at least 3 in task completion, organisation, intelligibility/comprehensibility and appropriate language on two sets at least 7 days apart. |
| A | Complete source synthesis, a presentation with follow-up discussion and two extended written genres for different audiences. Two qualified assessors score at least 3 on every dimension, resolving any difference greater than one point; parallel task dates differ by at least 7 days. |
| R | Complete a domain portfolio with two source syntheses, a meeting/negotiation, a presentation with unscripted questions, and two polished documents for distinct purposes. Two qualified assessors including a domain specialist score each dimension at least 3 on two portfolio cycles at least 14 days apart. No automated C2 certification is issued. |

For B through R, a delayed sample must include at least five eligible taught targets after seven days without immediate model exposure. At least four of those five are used or understood independently; performance is reported with its small denominator. A failed sub-gate assigns targeted practice rather than restarting the stage. A learner who declines one modality receives a skill profile and can continue available learning; full all-skill graduation remains unassigned. Time, vocabulary counts and tapping success never substitute for these tasks. Pilot analysis can change thresholds only in a new assessment-policy version, preserving old evidence and labels.

## Curriculum hierarchy

The authoritative structure is `curriculum.json`: course → stage → unit → module → lesson. Each of seven stages contains four units. Each unit contains an input/language module and a use/transfer module. Each module contains four lesson specifications, giving 28 units, 56 modules and 224 authoring assignments. Each lesson has an objective, target kinds, activity recipe, evidence contract, required media and explicit prerequisites.

| Stage | Unit 1 | Unit 2 | Unit 3 | Unit 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P | Meaning and sound awareness | Fidäl family relationships | Familiar picture words | First connected meanings |
| B | Meeting and introducing | Home and everyday actions | Needs and simple exchanges | Short messages and stories |
| E | Routine plans and places | Describing and comparing | Events and short narratives | Community interactions |
| I | Experiences and explanations | Managing situations | Stories and viewpoints | Connected projects |
| U | Discussion and decisions | Media and interpretation | Negotiation and collaboration | Arguments and reports |
| A | Complex narratives and literature | Source synthesis | Register and audience | Extended presentations and writing |
| R | Professional interaction | Specialist reading and listening | Academic and professional writing | Domain portfolio |

Within the input module, lesson 1 establishes meaning, lesson 2 notices a relevant language/script pattern, lesson 3 retrieves after intervening work and lesson 4 applies the target to new input. Within the use module, lesson 1 rehearses production, lesson 2 completes a functional task, lesson 3 revisits the objective in a story/dialogue and lesson 4 performs a cold transfer checkpoint. Stories, dialogues, reviews and challenges are content types referenced by lessons, not separate reward activities.

The default route follows stage and unit order. Dependencies are objective-specific: a reading lesson requires relevant form knowledge, while an oral lesson need not. The manifest contains the default route and skill-specific prerequisites; placement may satisfy a prerequisite through evidence. Browsing is allowed, but a learner sees an optional prerequisite model before unsupported testing. A prerequisite that lacks reviewed content makes that activity unavailable rather than blocking all oral or literacy practice.

A challenge is a meaningful variation of a taught situation with one changed detail. A mastery check uses a parallel prompt with no answer visible. A review samples a previously taught target and records whether help was supplied. A story's target references allow vocabulary recycling to be checked automatically. A dialogue references functions, roles and reviewed acceptable responses. Longer words are allowed beyond the existing two-character Unit 2; the original Unit 2 bank and its exact-length rule remain unchanged.

### Current implementation mapping

The current Fidäl catalogue is source material for P unit 2; its visual difference checks establish visual discrimination only. The 32 picture candidates are source material for P unit 3 and require native spelling, meaning and image review. The remaining dictionary bank entries stay in reviewer tools. The 293 form inventory is a reference catalogue, not a requirement to learn every historical or additional form before using a word. A native literacy reviewer selects the common-form teaching order; existing catalogue order is not silently promoted to a frequency curriculum.

All new lesson specifications remain `blocked_content`. No placeholder translation, synthetic pronunciation or pending association becomes a learner lesson through manifest generation. `sourceMap` records the existing source paths, limitations and intended reuse without claiming formal curriculum completion.

## Review algorithm

### State and evidence

A review target is keyed by `(learner, language, contentId, contentVersion, skill, taskKind)`. Recognising a picture-word pair, hearing a word, spelling it, producing it and using it in conversation have distinct states. A major sense or answer change creates a new review state; old evidence remains historically attached to its version.

State contains `step`, `dueAt`, `lastAttemptAt`, `lastExposureAt`, `lastColdAt`, `coldCorrect`, `coldTotal`, `delayedCorrect`, `delayedTotal`, `transferSum`, `transferTotal`, `lastSessionId`, `lastOutcome` and `schedulerVersion`. Events record stable idempotency ID, target key, time, session ID, outcome, support used, whether a model appeared first, task version, assessor type and optional transfer rubric score. No raw recording is necessary to schedule review.

A scored cold event has no help or pre-answer model and is either a first encounter without prior exposure or at least 24 hours after the last exposure. Same-day attempts remain practice evidence. An independent event has no help and no pre-answer model. A delayed event is independent, occurs at least 24 hours after the last exposure, and is the first scored event for that target in the session. An interval may advance only when an independent correct event is due. An immediate retry can support teaching but cannot increase spacing, mastery evidence or a retention estimate. Seven-day retention reports use a stricter gap and appear separately from the 24-hour scheduler eligibility flag.

### Deterministic scheduler version 2

The starting intervals are 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days after the qualifying attempt. New targets start at step 0 after introduction. An independent due correct attempt advances one step, capped at step 5. An incorrect or helped attempt sets step 0 and due tomorrow; it also offers one within-session corrective revisit after at least two intervening tasks where available. The corrective revisit is teaching evidence only. Skipping leaves the state unchanged and temporarily removes that target from the session queue.

Teaching without a scored answer updates `lastExposureAt`, which prevents a subsequent check from being called cold. Same-day repeated correct answers do not extend the due date. Earlier device timestamps do not move state backwards; the UI explains clock uncertainty and retains the last valid state. The reference reducer rejects invalid timestamps and event fields. An event ID is consumed once; offline imports de-duplicate before applying the reducer in `(time,id)` order, then verify a deterministic state hash.

For word, phrase and sentence review, the target includes both sense/function and modality. Story review selects a different comprehension prompt, a retell or a related passage rather than memorised quiz order. Pronunciation review requires a reviewed recording and a valid intelligibility judgement; absent that judgement it schedules practice without a scored correctness claim. Grammar review changes vocabulary/context while preserving the reviewed target construction. Conversation review samples functional goals with new details; an AI sentiment or similarity score cannot count as success.

### Scores

Observed retention is `100 × delayedCorrect / delayedTotal`, accompanied by numerator, denominator, time-gap band and task type. Display no percentage below five eligible delayed events. Predicted retention remains null until a model is calibrated against held-out Tigrinya learner data. No exponential forgetting formula is presented as a measured probability.

Internal evidence score is `100 × (0.4 × coldCorrect/coldTotal + 0.3 × delayedCorrect/delayedTotal + 0.3 × transferSum/(4 × transferTotal))`, rounded to an integer. It is null until there are at least three cold events, two delayed events and two transfer tasks with at least seven days between the first and latest cold events. A high score does not establish stage graduation. Reports show the components, not a universal “percent fluent.”

### Queue and adaptation

Eligible approved targets are filtered by skill, accessibility, current pack version and available assets. Sort due targets by oldest due time, then by last helped/incorrect status, then stable ID. Select no more than the session's review-task budget and no more than two consecutive tasks in one modality. Overflow stays due for another session with no warning count. New material starts only when the selected review budget is met or the learner explicitly chooses new learning.

Suggested 3/5/10/15/30-minute review budgets are 2/3/5/7/10 short prompts, with 0/1/2/2/3 new targets respectively. Long conversations replace several short prompts and use an estimated duration, not these count limits. These are planning budgets; the interface has no countdown and honours an immediate stop. The current runtime's three-minute one-word activity remains a guided alternative until cold review integration passes its migration gate.

After two independent failures on the same objective across separate sessions, offer a worked model and a simpler contrast. After independent due successes, vary context before increasing linguistic difficulty. Any model shown is recorded as exposure. Adaptation changes the activity, not the answer key or approved learning objective.

Spaced practice has benefits for second-language learning, while effects depend on task and retention interval; the product's exact schedule requires evaluation. [Kim and Webb, 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12479)

## Story progression

Vocabulary estimates below are budgets for unique sense entries within one work; length is a separate control. Reviewers also assess syntax, topic familiarity, inference and script load. A low word count alone does not make a text easy.

| Format and stage | Objective | Initial size budget | Complexity and audio contract | Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Picture story P | Follow a sequence and connect spoken meaning with images | 10–25 senses; 3–6 panels | One event per panel; reviewed narration; text optional | Sequence pictures and identify meaning without English reading |
| Early reader B | Decode taught patterns in connected meaning | 25–60 senses; 50–150 running words | Controlled forms and recurring phrases; full narration, replay per sentence | Read a new passage and answer meaning questions |
| Graded reader E | Read/listen independently with limited support | 80–200 senses; 200–600 running words | Short linked events; aligned narration and optional glossary | Explain a cause or outcome |
| Chapter story I | Sustain understanding over episodes | 300–800 senses; 1,000–4,000 running words | More than one viewpoint; chapter audio and place saving | Retell and locate evidence across chapters |
| Native story U/A | Interpret voice and context | Editorial analysis; no fixed unique-word quota | Licensed authentic text; optional professional audio | Compare interpretations with evidence |
| Advanced article A/R | Evaluate argument, framing and sources | Domain-specific; assessed before release | Minimal adaptation; source metadata; audio where licensed | Synthesis and critical response |
| News U/R | Understand a dated account and distinguish claim/source | Short accessible item through full report | Publication/event dates and context; licensed clips | Identify what is known, attributed or uncertain |
| Podcast U/R | Follow extended natural speech | Segment-specific targets | Multiple reviewed speakers; chapter markers; verified transcript | Summary and stance/detail tasks |
| Audiobook I/R | Sustain listening and narrative comprehension | Level-appropriate work | Licensed chapters; transcript/text where rights allow | Retell beyond a memorised segment |

Every work stores introduced targets, recycled targets and unknown-target estimates. Beginner reading aims for at least 95% familiar running tokens after reviewed gloss support; independent extensive reading aims toward 98%. These are editorial starting criteria, not Tigrinya-validated comprehension guarantees. A human reviewer can lower difficulty by rewriting or splitting a work; the engine never rewrites approved text at delivery time.

Narration first plays at a natural, clear pace. A slower version is a separately reviewed take where possible. Any technical time stretching must preserve intelligibility and be reviewed; it is not a new pronunciation model. Glosses, replay and transcripts are learning support, and their use is tracked only locally when saving is chosen.

## Speaking progression

| Step | Learner task | Support removal | Advancement evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Meaningful imitation | Hear and repeat a useful reviewed expression | Picture and recording remain available | Relevant production; no automated accent score |
| 2 Sound distinctions | Notice and rehearse reviewed meaningful contrasts | Contrast model, then isolated response | Human-reviewed intelligibility where scoring is required |
| 3 Read aloud | Connect introduced script with speech | Word model to sentence text | Correctly conveys taught meanings; separate from spontaneous speaking |
| 4 Sentence practice | Respond with a taught pattern and changed detail | Selectable supports, then no visible response | Two relevant independent responses |
| 5 Guided speaking | Describe a scene or experience | Optional planning picture/keywords | Task completion rubric at least 3 |
| 6 Roleplay | Accomplish a practical exchange | Model exchange, then only scenario goal | Handles changed information and one clarification |
| 7 Open conversation | Maintain an exchange on a familiar topic | Optional planning, no fixed script | Relevant turns, repair and sustained meaning |
| 8 Debate and presentation | Argue, organise and respond to questions | Outline allowed; no supplied answer | Audience/task rubric, human-rated |
| 9 Professional speaking | Negotiate, facilitate and present specialist information | Authentic reference material allowed | Domain expert and language assessor agree on functional success |

Learners privately rehearse before recording or sharing. Listening back compares the intended message with the model and offers one actionable improvement. Feedback during a fluency turn is delayed until the turn ends unless communication breaks down; pronunciation drills can receive immediate feedback. No required public performance, public ranking or mandatory microphone access is introduced.

## Listening progression

Difficulty changes one main dimension at a time: utterance length, linguistic complexity, speaker familiarity, number of speakers, contextual support or acoustic conditions. Slower and normal recordings use the same reviewed meaning. Natural/native speech is not a separate level of correctness; all models should be authentic from the beginning, with increasing demands over time.

P/B uses short clear speech, familiar meanings and visual context. E adds normal-paced short dialogues and removes answer-leading pictures. I adds longer turns, multiple speakers and practical inference. U adds new speakers, varied registers and reviewed varieties. A/R adds long authentic discourse, implicit stance and specialist contexts. Optional realistic noise begins only after the clean equivalent is understood; it is never required for users with auditory access needs.

Assess comprehension before transcripts are revealed. Replay, slower takes and transcripts mark supported attempts, not failures. Record gist, detail, inference and source attribution separately. Recommend the lowest missing prerequisite dimension: difficulty with speaker changes leads to another familiar-content speaker, not an automatic vocabulary demotion. No accent is labelled inferior. Variety metadata makes the course's coverage visible.

## Writing progression

| Stage | Task | Required evidence for moving on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form observation and tracing | Follow a reviewed guide or inspect static steps | Can locate relevant features; tracing accuracy is not a scored literacy outcome |
| Copying | Copy familiar forms and words | Four of five samples recognisable to a qualified reviewer using an accepted-style rubric |
| Recall and typing | Produce a taught word from meaning or dictation with approved audio | Eight of ten taught-pattern items independently correct on two dates |
| Sentence construction | Build then independently write a meaningful sentence | Meaning conveyed in four of five unseen prompts; accepted variants respected |
| Paragraph | Describe or recount connected information | Rubric 3 in meaning, sequence and comprehensibility |
| Journal | Write personally relevant entries | Revision improves a selected issue; personal disclosure is optional |
| Essay | Develop a position using reasons and evidence | Rubric 3 in task fulfilment, organisation, language and source use |
| Formal writing | Adapt register, purpose and audience | Human-reviewed appropriateness and clarity at rubric 3 |
| Professional writing | Produce authentic domain documents | Domain and language review at rubric 3; no unverified professional advice is taught |

Fidäl models preserve legitimate handwriting variants. Printed font outlines do not establish stroke order. All claimed starts/directions require sourced, licensed teacher data with native review. Keyboard entry supports composition events and native layouts; non-Latin input is tested with real devices. Autocorrect is optional during writing practice and disabled or disclosed for spelling assessments. Original and revised drafts remain private local artefacts unless deliberately shared.

## Culture curriculum

Culture content is attached to a language function and a source. Food connects to describing, preferences and sequences; traditions and celebrations to invitations, stories and questions; family life and etiquette to relationships, requests and register. History connects to chronology, attribution and comparing accounts. Music connects to listening, metaphor and context only where lyrics/recording rights allow. Religion is optional, descriptive and age-appropriate, with clearly attributed perspectives. Regional and diaspora differences appear throughout the course rather than as exceptions to a single supposed standard.

Each cultural work identifies the represented community/region, contributor, date or period where relevant, variation notes, age suitability, sources and learning targets. A qualified language reviewer and a community/context reviewer approve the exact version. Contested accounts are attributed and compared, not flattened into a single answer. Learners may skip a topic without losing a language level.

Retention is supported by meaningful context and repeated language use: hear an account, understand key expressions, ask or answer a related question, and later reuse the language in a different situation. Cultural trivia points and guessing stereotypes are excluded from assessment.
